Amalfi Coast

The quiet fishing villages next door to Amalfi

6 min read Neighborhoods Cetara, Italy

Positano gets the postcards, and Amalfi town gets the day-trippers off the ferries. Both are worth seeing once. But drive twenty minutes past Amalfi in the other direction, toward Salerno, and the coast road drops into Cetara — a working fishing village that never bothered to dress up for tourists, because for a long time, almost none came.

A harbor that still works

Cetara's small crescent of a harbor is still full of actual fishing boats, not the pastel-painted photo props that line up in the bigger towns. The town's whole economy still runs on anchovies — Cetara is the source of colatura di alici, an amber fish sauce pressed from salted anchovies that's been made here since Roman times, and every restaurant in town serves something built around the catch that morning.

There's no boardwalk, no strip of matching umbrellas. Just a narrow beach, a watchtower on the hill above town, and a handful of trattorias where the pasta alla colatura — spaghetti tossed with that anchovy sauce, garlic, and parsley — costs half what you'd pay for a worse version in Positano.

What a half-day here looks like

Park above town — the streets below are narrow, mostly stairs — and walk down through the old center toward the water. Mid-morning, the harbor is busiest: boats coming in, nets being sorted on the dock, nothing staged for an audience. By early afternoon it quiets down into the kind of slow lunch the rest of the coast has mostly lost to volume.

Climb up to the Torre di Cetara, the old watchtower above the beach, for the view that every Amalfi postcard is chasing — coastline, cliffs, water — minus the crowd standing next to you to get it.

Worth knowing before you go

Cetara is a real town, not a resort — that means shorter hours. Many restaurants close for a few hours in the afternoon and some shut entirely on Mondays, so check ahead rather than showing up hopeful. It also means no real hotel scene; this is a half-day or dinner stop from a base in Amalfi or Salerno, not a place to sleep, which is exactly why it's stayed this quiet.
